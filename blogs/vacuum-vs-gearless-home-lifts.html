Walk into any home-lift showroom in India and you will hear two words repeated endlessly: vacuum and gearless. First, How Each Technology Actually Works

The Vacuum (Pneumatic) Lift

A vacuum lift — a category popularised in India by vendors like Nibav — is essentially a transparent, self-supporting tube. Turbines at the top of the tube pull air out of the space above the cabin, and normal atmospheric pressure below gently pushes the cabin upward. To descend, the system simply releases air back in, and the cabin floats down under controlled gravity. There are no cables, no counterweights and no hydraulic oil — the physics of air pressure does the lifting.

The MRL Gearless Lift

A Machine-Room-Less (MRL) gearless lift uses a compact permanent-magnet traction motor mounted inside the top of the shaft itself — which is why no separate machine room is needed. The motor drives the cabin directly, with no gearbox in between, which is exactly why the ride is so smooth and near-silent. This is the same drive philosophy used in premium high-rise elevators, shrunk and refined for the home. LEONI's gearless lifts are built with BIS/ISI-marked and CE-marked components; installation includes preparing a standard lift pit, with our team managing the civil work and sharing the exact timeline after a site survey.

Footprint and Civil Work

The vacuum tube's circular footprint slips into stairwell voids and double-height living rooms beautifully, and vacuum vendors market genuinely pit-free installation — a real advantage in a finished home. An MRL gearless lift skips the machine room, but it does use a standard lift pit: LEONI's team manages that civil work and confirms the exact scope after a site survey. If you are retrofitting an existing villa, both are worth evaluating; our complete guide to pitless home lifts explains which technologies can genuinely skip the pit, floor by floor.

Capacity: The Deciding Factor for Most Families

Here the technologies genuinely diverge. Air pressure can only lift so much, so vacuum lifts carry lighter loads — typically one to three passengers, with wheelchair-capable models only at the largest tube sizes. A gearless machine has no such ceiling at residential scale: it will carry a full family, a wheelchair with an attendant, or grandparents with luggage, day in and day out. If your lift is primarily for elderly parents or accessibility, capacity and door width usually settle the debate in favour of gearless.

Ride Feel, Noise and Power

A vacuum lift feels like floating — and its gravity descent uses almost no electricity, a genuinely elegant trick. The trade-off is the turbine hum on the way up, which is noticeable in a quiet home at night. A gearless drive is the opposite personality: whisper-quiet in both directions with a steady, planted ride, and a modest power draw comparable to common household appliances. Crucially for Indian conditions, every LEONI lift carries an Automatic Rescue Device (ARD) — a battery backup that glides you to the nearest floor and opens the door if the power cuts mid-ride.

Aesthetics: Sculpture vs Bespoke Interior

This is where hearts get involved. A panoramic vacuum tube is architectural jewellery — a transparent column your guests will photograph. A gearless cabin, by contrast, disappears into your interior scheme: LEONI cabins are finished in Titanium Gold, Royal Marble, Signature Glass or Classic Walnut, matched to your home rather than standing apart from it. One is a statement; the other is harmony. Neither answer is wrong.

Maintenance

Vacuum lifts have refreshingly few moving parts — no ropes, no counterweight — with the main recurring item being periodic replacement of the air-seal. Gearless traction is the most proven drive technology in the elevator world, and its residential maintenance is routine and predictable. In both cases, insist on a manufacturer with a real service network: a lift is a 15–20 year relationship, not a purchase.

The Decision Framework: Match the Lift to the Home

  • Compact duplex or G+1 retrofit, 1–2 daily users: a vacuum capsule's small circular footprint and vendor-advertised pit-free install can make it the least disruptive choice — verify the specifics with the vendor.
  • Family villa (G+2 / G+3) with children or elderly parents: MRL gearless — higher capacity, wider doors, silent night-time operation and ARD backup for power cuts.
  • Double-height living room or atrium that deserves a centrepiece: the panoramic vacuum tube is unmatched as visual drama.
  • Interiors already committed to a material palette: a gearless cabin in Titanium Gold, Royal Marble, Signature Glass or Classic Walnut will blend in as if the architect planned it — the route most of our clients take, from home lifts in Hyderabad to home lift installations in Vizag and Bangalore.
  • Wheelchair accessibility as the primary purpose: gearless, in almost every case — verify platform size and door clearance before anything else.
