Vacuum vs Gearless Home Lifts: Which Is Right for Your Home?

LEONI builds both. Here is the honest head-to-head we walk every client through before they choose.

Walk into any home-lift showroom in India and you will hear two words repeated endlessly: vacuum and gearless. One is a glass tube that rides on air; the other is a bespoke cabin driven by a compact magnetic motor. Both are pitless, both skip the machine room, and both can be running in your home in about 48 hours. So which one actually belongs in your home? Because LEONI Elevators manufactures both — our pneumatic Series V and our MRL gearless range — we have no reason to push you toward either. Here is the comparison as we give it to our own clients.

First, How Each Technology Actually Works

The Vacuum (Pneumatic) Lift

A vacuum lift — the technology behind our Series V and popularised in India by brands like Nibav — is essentially a transparent, self-supporting tube. Turbines at the top of the tube pull air out of the space above the cabin, and normal atmospheric pressure below gently pushes the cabin upward. To descend, the system simply releases air back in, and the cabin floats down under controlled gravity. There are no cables, no counterweights and no hydraulic oil — the physics of air pressure does the lifting.

The MRL Gearless Lift

A Machine-Room-Less (MRL) gearless lift uses a compact permanent-magnet traction motor mounted inside the top of the shaft itself — which is why no separate machine room is needed. The motor drives the cabin directly, with no gearbox in between, which is exactly why the ride is so smooth and near-silent. This is the same drive philosophy used in premium high-rise elevators, shrunk and refined for the home. LEONI's gearless lifts are TUV SUD certified and designed around a zero-pit structure, so no deep excavation is required.

Footprint and Civil Work

This is closer than most buyers expect. The vacuum tube's circular footprint slips into stairwell voids and double-height living rooms beautifully. But an MRL gearless lift no longer demands the deep pit and machine room of old-school elevators — LEONI's pitless structure needs no major civil work either. If you are retrofitting an existing villa, both are genuine options; our complete guide to pitless home lifts covers the structural details floor by floor.

Capacity: The Deciding Factor for Most Families

Here the technologies genuinely diverge. Air pressure can only lift so much, so vacuum lifts carry lighter loads — typically one to three passengers, with wheelchair-capable models only at the largest tube sizes. A gearless machine has no such ceiling at residential scale: it will carry a full family, a wheelchair with an attendant, or grandparents with luggage, day in and day out. If your lift is primarily for elderly parents or accessibility, capacity and door width usually settle the debate in favour of gearless.

Ride Feel, Noise and Power

A vacuum lift feels like floating — and its gravity descent uses almost no electricity, a genuinely elegant trick. The trade-off is the turbine hum on the way up, which is noticeable in a quiet home at night. A gearless drive is the opposite personality: whisper-quiet in both directions with a steady, planted ride, and a modest power draw comparable to common household appliances. Crucially for Indian conditions, every LEONI lift carries an Automatic Rescue Device (ARD) — a battery backup that glides you to the nearest floor and opens the door if the power cuts mid-ride.

Aesthetics: Sculpture vs Bespoke Interior

This is where hearts get involved. A panoramic vacuum tube is architectural jewellery — a transparent column your guests will photograph. A gearless cabin, by contrast, disappears into your interior scheme: LEONI cabins are finished in Titanium Gold, Royal Marble, Signature Glass or Classic Walnut, matched to your home rather than standing apart from it. One is a statement; the other is harmony. Neither answer is wrong.

Maintenance

Vacuum lifts have refreshingly few moving parts — no ropes, no counterweight — with the main recurring item being periodic replacement of the air-seal. Gearless traction is the most proven drive technology in the elevator world, and its residential maintenance is routine and predictable. In both cases, insist on a manufacturer with a real service network: a lift is a 15–20 year relationship, not a purchase.

The Decision Framework: Match the Lift to the Home

  • Compact duplex or G+1 retrofit, 1–2 daily users: the vacuum lift's small circular footprint and zero-pit install make it the least disruptive choice.
  • Family villa (G+2 / G+3) with children or elderly parents: MRL gearless — higher capacity, wider doors, silent night-time operation and ARD backup for power cuts.
  • Double-height living room or atrium that deserves a centrepiece: the panoramic vacuum tube is unmatched as visual drama.
  • Interiors already committed to a material palette: a gearless cabin in Titanium Gold, Royal Marble, Signature Glass or Classic Walnut will blend in as if the architect planned it — the route most of our clients take, from home lifts in Hyderabad to home lift installations in Vizag and Bangalore.
  • Wheelchair accessibility as the primary purpose: gearless, in almost every case — verify platform size and door clearance before anything else.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better for Indian homes — a vacuum lift or an MRL gearless lift?

Neither is universally better. A vacuum lift wins on footprint and sculptural looks for one or two passengers, while an MRL gearless lift wins on capacity, cabin customisation and quietness during travel. The right choice depends on who will ride the lift every day and how your home is built.

Do vacuum and gearless home lifts work during power cuts?

Yes. A vacuum lift can descend gently under gravity even without power, and every LEONI lift — Series V and MRL gearless alike — includes an Automatic Rescue Device (ARD) battery backup that carries the cabin to the nearest floor and opens the door during a power cut.

How much space does each type of home lift need?

Vacuum lifts typically need a circular footprint of roughly 1 to 1.5 metres in diameter with zero pit. LEONI MRL gearless lifts are also pitless and typically fit a compact shaft of roughly 1.2 × 1.2 metres for a family-size cabin. Neither technology needs a machine room.

How long does installation take for a pitless home lift?

Because both LEONI technologies are pitless and machine-room-less, a typical installation completes in about 48 hours with no major civil work — for both vacuum and gearless models.
